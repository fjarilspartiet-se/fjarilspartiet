Fjärilspartiet appears to be playing a different game.

The strategist's challenge becomes:

# A useful distinction

I think Fjärilspartiet should avoid becoming:

### A solution party

because then you become one more competitor among many.

But you must also avoid becoming:

### A process-only party

because voters need tangible outcomes.

The sweet spot is:

### A capability party

Your message becomes:

> We strengthen society's ability to solve problems.

Examples:

Healthcare:

* not "our ideology says X"
* not "we have the perfect solution"

Instead:

> We want healthcare systems that continuously learn from practitioners, patients, data and outcomes.

Housing:

> We want governance structures capable of rapidly discovering and scaling what works.

Education:

> We want educational systems that adapt to changing realities rather than defending outdated models.

This is politically distinct.

# The anti-polarization strategy

This is where things become especially interesting.

Most political systems reward conflict.

Conflict generates:

* attention
* media coverage
* group identity
* fundraising
* loyalty

Fjärilspartiet appears to reject that mechanism.

Strategically this is both noble and dangerous.

Because conflict is politically efficient.

* friend/enemy

* integration
* learning
* perspective-taking
* synthesis

That requires more cognitive effort.
